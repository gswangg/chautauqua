# DEC-441: Amendment (wave 135): The reminder email is built to its frame: greeting, nudge, 'Still to do', one CTA, signature -- and one builder still serves both preview and send

## Amendment (wave 110): the reminder review dialog shows what each person owes and what they will read -- the drafts carry the tasks, never a re-parse of the body

docs/design/Chautauqua Speakers.dc.html:492-541 draws, per recipient, a 1fr/auto row: name over that person's outstanding TASK NAMES, with an overdue/due-soon flag right-flushed; then a `What <name> will read` eyebrow over a rendered email card; then the caption `Each speaker's list is their own · the body is built from their outstanding tasks, not typed`; then `Logged in Comms history` beside Send/Cancel. Live renders a bare `<ul>` of `name · email` and one `<pre>`. RULED: previewRemindNow's draft shape gains the per-recipient task list built from the SAME `group.assignments` buildReminderMessage consumes -- never re-derived, never scraped back out of `text` (a second derivation of one fact is DEC-613's trap). The overdue/due-soon vocabulary is the existing one in app/src/pages/speakers/overdue.ts, not a third phrasing.

## Amendment (wave 135): The reminder email is built to its frame: greeting, nudge, 'Still to do', one CTA, signature -- and one builder still serves both preview and send

Chautauqua Speakers.dc.html:515-535 draws the reminder email in full, and the tree ships a different message: buildReminderMessage emits 'You have outstanding tasks for {event}:' + task lines + a bare portal URL, and sendReminderEmails calls renderEmailHtml with no `cta` at all. So the last desktop control-label gap is closed by using a shell affordance that has been sitting unused, not by inventing markup. The frame's greeting, its count-composed nudge sentence, its 'Still to do' section label and its organiser signature come with it, because a frame is adopted whole or not at all. DEC-441's wave-110 invariant is preserved unchanged: buildReminderMessage stays the ONE builder so previewRemindNow and the real send remain byte-identical, and the plain-text part keeps the portal URL in its own body (the shell's paste-line is HTML-only, and a text-only client must still be able to reach the portal).

The frame's rendered-email card at :516-533 is a SEPARATE and larger gap (RemindPreviewModal.tsx still ships `<pre>{first.text}</pre>`) that needs a structured server contract and is deliberately NOT in this wave's scope.

## Amendment (wave 6): ResponseModal's frame subtitle names the assignment's own createdAt as the SENT date, surfaced on the DEC-291 wire as `assignedAt`

Chautauqua Speakers.dc.html:235 draws `Priya Raman · sent 2 Apr` under the answered form-kind task modal; ResponseModal.tsx renders `Completed {formatDate(detail.completedAt)}`. DEC-441 already ruled the frame right — the date that makes a stale response legible is when the organiser ACTED, and completion is already implied by the answers being present — but never named WHICH stored fact is 'sent'. `task_assignment` carries no sent column; it carries `createdAt` (the moment the task went out to this contact), `completedAt`, and `lastRemindedAt`. Ruled: `createdAt` of the assignment row IS the sent date. `getAssignmentResponseDetail` selects `schema.taskAssignment.createdAt` and returns it as `assignedAt: number` on `AssignmentResponseDetail`; the modal renders `{contactName} · sent {formatDate(detail.assignedAt)}`. `lastRemindedAt` is explicitly NOT it — a reminder is not the send — and no new column is minted for a fact the table already holds (DEC-851/DEC-988: never a declared knob with no reader, and never a second source for an existing one).

## Amendment (wave 7): The reminder preview renders a structured card built from the send's own parts — and its header is the SUBJECT, never a fabricated letterhead

Speakers.dc.html:491 draws a rendered email card; RemindPreviewModal.tsx ships `<pre>{first.text}</pre>`. `buildReminderMessage` (src/server/repo/tasks/reminders.ts:391) ALREADY computes every part the frame draws as a local — greeting (:422), nudge (:409), taskLines (:415), portalLine (:418), signature (:419) — and throws them away into a joined string. The card's header band instead carries the `subject` `buildReminderMessage` already returns — which today's `<pre>` drops entirely, so this makes the preview strictly MORE honest, not less.


## Amendment (wave 32): the email shell's highlighted-fact block is drawn on every email the pack depicts, and the product ships none of them

docs/design/Chautauqua Comms.dc.html's 'What each template renders' frame draws, inside the 560px card between the body and the CTA, a border-left accent block (`border-left:3px solid #4E5C31; padding:2px 0 2px 15px`) holding an uppercase label over a value. It is guarded by `sc-if value="{{ e.hasFact }}"` with `hint-placeholder-val="{{ true }}"` — and the frame's own `emails` array sets `hasFact: true` on ALL FOUR templates: 'Your talk', 'Reviewer feedback', 'Still to do', 'Your slot'. So this is a drawn element of the email shell, not a rare conditional. src/mail/shell.ts's `EmailShellOptions` is `{ eventName, reason, cta }` — no fact field exists, so every email Chautauqua sends ships without the block, on desktop and phone alike (an email has no viewport to freeze). RULED: BUILD it on the shell as `fact?: { label, value } | null`, rendered between body and CTA, escaping through escapeHtml and spending only colours already in that file's constants (#4E5C31 is CTA_BG, #565A4B is MUTED_COLOR, #1B1D17 is MASTHEAD_INK) so PERMITTED_MAIL_COLOURS gains no member; absent `fact`, the emitted bytes are byte-identical to today. Wire the caller the frame names by its own label: the task-reminder email is the frame's `factLabel: 'Still to do'`, and DEC-441's wave-135 amendment already rules that email is built to this frame. DEC-441's wave-110 invariant is untouched — buildReminderMessage stays the ONE builder so preview and send remain byte-identical, and the plain-text part keeps the task lines in its own body exactly as the shell's paste-URL line is HTML-only.


## Amendment (wave 38): the mail shell's fact VALUE is one run — a caller composing a multi-item fact joins it in the frame's own sentence register, never with a newline HTML collapses

The lens filed this as a shell defect (bare escapeHtml where textToHtml belongs). Opening the frame settles it the other way: docs/design/Chautauqua Comms.dc.html:719-723 draws the fact block as `display:flex; flex-direction:column; gap:5px` holding exactly TWO spans — the label over the value — and the frame's own demo data (:900-902) sets `factLabel: 'Still to do'` with `fact: 'Upload headshot — was due 10 April. Complete your bio — due 10 April.'`, a single period-terminated sentence run with no bullet prefixes. The 5px column gap is between LABEL and VALUE, not between task lines, and src/mail/shell.ts's two-row table with `padding-top:5px` is a correct translation of it. The real divergence is at the only production call site: src/server/repo/tasks/reminders.ts passes `parts.stillToDo.join("\n")`, and those lines carry the plain-text body's `- ` bullet prefix — so a two-task reminder renders in HTML as one run-on line whose separators have vanished (`- Slides — due 1 May - Bio — due 10 April`), which is neither the frame's sentence run nor a list. No test catches it because test/mail-shell-frame.test.ts only ever passes single-line fact values. RULED: the shell's fact value stays a single escaped run (the frame draws one span; textToHtml's <br> would invent a second row the frame does not draw); the CALLER composes the value in the frame's register — the same task data, without the plain-text bullet prefix, period-terminated and space-joined — and a test exercises a multi-item fact end to end so the collapse can never return silently.


## Amendment (wave 47): the HTML reminder email is COMPOSED to its frame — the shell body carries only what the frame draws above the fact block, and the sign-off gets its own shell slot after the CTA

PROVEN by rendering the real bytes (renderEmailHtml with the live reminder text, fact and cta): the sent HTML reads greeting / nudge / "Still to do" / the two bulleted task lines / the bare claim URL / the sign-off / "Still to do" AGAIN / the same two tasks again / the CTA button / "Or paste this into your browser: <same URL>". Every task appears twice, the section label appears twice, the claim URL appears twice, and the sign-off lands mid-message BEFORE the CTA. docs/design/Chautauqua Speakers.dc.html:522-531 draws each element exactly once, in the order greeting / nudge / Still-to-do / CTA / paste / sign-off. This is the wave-32 amendment's own residue: `fact` was ADDED on top of a body that already carried the list ("absent fact, the emitted bytes are byte-identical to today") and nobody removed the duplicate. A comment in test/reminders-timezone.test.ts rationalised it as "the HTML body above it legitimately renders the plain-text bulleted block verbatim" — that is a claim in a test, not a ruling, and the frame outranks it (DEC-380/DEC-582: frame-in-repo is the geometry authority). RULED: (a) the HTML body argument is the frame's own composition, greeting + nudge, nothing else; "Still to do" comes from `fact` alone and the portal link from `cta` alone; (b) src/mail/shell.ts gains `signOff?: string | null`, rendered as one escaped row BETWEEN ctaBlock and the footer — the frame draws the sign-off after the paste line, and the shell had no slot for it, which is why it could only reach the reader by riding inside the body; it spends BODY_COLOR (#3F4237, already declared, matching frame :531 exactly) so PERMITTED_MAIL_COLOURS gains no member, and an absent signOff emits byte-identical bytes; (c) the plain-text `text` part is UNCHANGED — the wave-135 amendment's clause that a text-only client must still read the list and reach the portal stands, and the two registers keep differing deliberately.

## Amendment (wave 48): the outbound email's reason clause is one vocabulary with one owner, in the frame's sentence register

src/mail/shell.ts renders `You are receiving this because ${reason}` and adds no terminal punctuation, so the footer sentence every recipient reads is composed entirely at the call site -- and there are eight call sites, each hand-writing its own string with no owner. Measured at main 01e1d12e2 they disagree on both axes the frame settles: docs/design/Chautauqua Comms.dc.html's four worked `why` values are all complete sentences that end in a full stop AND name the event ('You are receiving this because you submitted a talk to DevFlow Conf 2027.'), while in the tree only src/routes/auth-reset.tsx and src/routes/public/submit-post.tsx terminate the sentence -- six of eight outbound emails ship a footer sentence with no full stop -- and only three of eight name the event. This is DEC-613's trap at its eighth copy, and no instrument sees it: the reason is a plain string parameter, so nothing grades what a call site puts there. RULED: (a) src/mail/reasons.ts becomes the ONE composer, exporting a named builder per send path that takes the event name (and whatever that path needs) and returns a period-terminated, event-naming clause in the frame's register; (b) no renderEmailHtml call site may pass a string literal or template literal as `reason` -- a scan enumerates the call sites from disk (never a hand list, with a vacuous-population tripwire on the exact count) and fails on a ninth hand-written one; (c) a path with no event name in scope still returns a whole sentence, never a bare fragment; (d) the scan exports no ceiling -- the assertion is exact, so it needs no ratchet and no edit to the contended exit-predicate scope registry.

## Amendment (wave 49): the submission-received email is the pack's first worked email and the product ships a different message: it is adopted whole, and its claim URL stops shipping twice

docs/design/Chautauqua Comms.dc.html's `emails` data (the "Emails . What each template renders . One shell . 560px measure . no images" exhibit) draws four worked emails; this decision's wave-47 amendment proved and repaired the third (the reminder). The FIRST -- "Submission received", trigger "On submit . best-effort, never blocks the save", which fires from src/routes/public/submit-post.tsx -- has never been graded. Reading the frame beside the call site: the frame draws subject "We have your talk for DevFlow Conf 2027", greeting "Hi Dana,", a body that reassures ("Thanks for submitting ... Nothing else is needed from you right now -- the committee reads everything after the form closes."), a fact block labelled "Your talk" carrying the talk descriptor, a CTA labelled "Open your portal", a paste-the-URL line, a sign-off and the why clause. The product sends subject "We received your submission: {title}", a two-sentence body that INLINES the raw claim URL as its own paragraph, no fact block at all, and a CTA labelled "Claim your account to view your submission" / "Sign in to view your submission". Because the shell already renders "Or paste this into your browser: <href>" under the button, the claim URL ships TWICE in every confirmation email this product has ever sent -- the exact duplicate-URL defect wave-47 found one file over, in a second file nobody looked at. Grepping the tree for "Open your portal", "We have your talk" and "Nothing else is needed" returns nothing, so none of the frame's strings exist anywhere. RULED: (a) the HTML body argument is the frame's own composition -- greeting plus the reassurance sentence, nothing else; the portal link reaches the reader through `cta` alone, never inside the body; (b) the subject adopts the frame's shape, composed from the event name, not the talk title; (c) the fact block is passed with the frame's label "Your talk" and a value composed from the data that actually exists at that call site (the title, plus the selected track names when any) -- where a component the frame draws has no data behind it, that is named in one line at the call site rather than invented, per DEC-037 clause (c); (d) the CTA label adopts the frame's words; (e) the composition moves into a pure module so the shape is testable without a request, and the plain-text `text` part keeps a reachable portal URL for a text-only client, unchanged in principle from the wave-135 clause.
