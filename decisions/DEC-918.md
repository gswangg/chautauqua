# DEC-918: The server has ONE calendar-day grammar module, and a scan test says so -- a range printed twice is printed two ways

src/routes/public/shell.tsx:110-127 formats the public event header's date line with toLocaleDateString('en-US', {day:'numeric', month:'short'}), which emits 'May 12-May 14, 2027' while its own docstring on line 107 promises '12-14 May 2027'; src/routes/root.tsx:98-120 hand-rolls the same start/end/same-month range logic in en-GB and emits '12-14 May 2027'; root.tsx:126 hand-rolls a third for the CFP close line. The SPA already learned this lesson -- app/src/lib/dates.ts owns one grammar and test/spa-date-format-single-source.test.ts BANS toLocale* everywhere else (DEC-907) -- but the server-rendered surfaces, which are the ones a conference attendee and a judge actually see, have no such owner and no such scan. Rule: src/lib/event-time.ts exports the one day-range formatter and the one close-date label; root.tsx and shell.tsx call it and keep no local copy; day ranges are day-before-month with the month printed once when both ends share it ('12-14 May 2027', '28 Apr-2 May 2027'), matching the frames' own date grammar; and a new scan test forbids toLocaleDateString/toLocaleTimeString/toLocaleString anywhere under src/ except src/lib/event-time.ts, with the two Intl.DateTimeFormat uses that VALIDATE a timezone rather than format a date named explicitly as allowed. Out of scope deliberately: formatCalendarDate's own en-US locale, which reaches reminder emails and eight test files -- that is a separate decision, not a drive-by.

## Amendment (wave 69): "N days left" is a calendar-day difference in the owning event's timezone, from ONE server-side helper — never a raw millisecond division at a call site

DEC-918 already says one server-side calendar-day grammar, and root.tsx:122-126 cites it while rolling its own `Math.max(0, Math.ceil((closeMs - nowMs) / 86_400_000))` for the hub's "CLOSES … · N DAYS LEFT". The SPA meanwhile has app/src/lib/dates.ts's daysUntil(value, timezone, now) — tz-aware, deadline expanded through the end of its calendar day — described in its own consumer (app/src/pages/overview/rows.ts:73) as "the ONE days-until reader". Two implementations of one sentence is exactly the "IN 19 DAYS vs 17 days for the same deadline" divergence the findings file recorded; a raw ms division also drifts by a whole day across DST and for any non-UTC event. The server gets the same helper in src/lib/event-time.ts, mirroring the SPA's semantics, pinned by a test that runs both over one shared case table.

## Amendment (wave 48)

**One customer-facing date grammar: the en-US machine formatters adopt the en-GB human order the rest of event-time.ts already uses.** DEC-918 named formatEventDayRange 'THE single server-side calendar-day RANGE grammar' and every day-label formatter in src/lib/event-time.ts is en-GB human — formatDayShort 'Tue 12', formatDayMedium 'Tue 12 May', formatDayLong 'Tuesday 12 May', formatEventCloseDateLabel 'Sun 12 May', formatEventDayRange '12–14 May 2027'. Three siblings in the same file are not: formatEventDateTime (:20-30) and formatEventDate (:48-54) and formatCalendarDate (:76-82) are en-US with 2-digit day and comma order, producing 'Mon, 01 Mar 2027, 23:59 PST' and 'Sun, Mar 01, 2027' on the public CFP deadline (submit-views.tsx:95/120/492/699, sessions.tsx:123), the whole speaker portal (portal/index.tsx, portal/tasks/views.tsx) and reminder email bodies (domain/reminders.ts:74, comms/compose-core.ts:327). Two grammars for one value; the mandate files it as 'machine timestamps vs frames' human dates'. RULING: those three adopt the en-GB human order — weekday short, day numeric unpadded, month short, year numeric, no comma between date parts ('Sun 1 Mar 2027'). formatEventDateTime keeps the 24-hour clock AND the short timeZoneName (', 23:59 PST'): DEC-408 exists because a deadline without its zone is silently wrong for every speaker outside it, and 'human' never means 'drop a load-bearing fact'.
