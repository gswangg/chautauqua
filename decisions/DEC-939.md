# DEC-939: A rating scale is a radio group of EQUAL segments filled in brand, read at the product measure, and the conflict declaration comes AFTER the work

Six defects on one screen, all one posture. (1) Scorecard.tsx's root is `chq-page chq-review-page` and `.chq-review-page` (review.css:14) declares no max-width, so the scorecard renders full-bleed while every other single-column surface clamps to `--chq-measure`. (2) `.chq-review-rating-group` is a wrapping flex row of `min-width:40px` chips, not the frame's five equal segments spanning the measure. (3) `.chq-review-rating-btn-selected` fills `--chq-ink`; the frame fills brand olive, the family every other selected control in the product uses. (4) The recusal block renders at Scorecard.tsx:351, ABOVE the criteria — the reviewer is asked to declare a conflict before being shown what they would be conflicted about. (5) That control is a `<label className="chq-review-checkbox-label">` whose only input is `<input type="text">`: there is no checkbox, and the reason field's accessible name is the sentence "I have a conflict of interest with this submission". (6) `.chq-review-criterion.chq-focused` paints a full sunk band across whichever criterion holds focus, which in a static frame reads as one criterion styled unlike its siblings. Rule: the scorecard page clamps to `--chq-measure`; the rating group becomes `grid-template-columns: repeat(N, 1fr)` across that measure with the selected segment filled `--chq-brand`/`--chq-on-brand`; the group KEEPS role=radio + aria-checked and does NOT gain aria-pressed — a single-select scale is a radio group, not a set of toggle buttons, and this decision closes that gate item as refused-with-reason rather than leaving it to be re-filed; the recusal block moves BELOW the comment field and above the actions; the conflict control becomes a real `<input type="checkbox">` that REVEALS the optional reason field only when checked (DEC-883's toggle grammar); the focus affordance becomes an inset ring, never a fill. The reviewer queue's existing progress bar gains the frame's "N of M done" caption from the two counts it already computes.

## Amendment (wave 39): a scoped rule never restates the colour of an element that carries a button-face class, and the reviewer's one primary action is contrast-tested

DEC-939 put the review surface's fills on brand tokens and made a rating scale measurable. The queue's single primary action escaped: `a.chq-review-queue-score-action.chq-btn.chq-btn-primary` (app/src/pages/review/ReviewerQueue.tsx:151) is painted by a later, equal-specificity rule (review.css:451-456) whose `color: var(--chq-accent, var(--chq-ink))` beats `.chq-btn-primary`'s cream — ink on olive, 2.40:1, on the reviewer's only way into a scorecard. The same `.chq-btn-primary` on the scorecard is correct, so this is not a token problem, it is a scoping problem: a page-scoped rule adjusted geometry and helped itself to colour on the way past. Ruling: a face class owns colour and background; a scoped rule may set size, weight, spacing and width on a button-face element but never `color` or `background`. The guard is a parsed-CSS contrast test in the shape of app/src/pages/agenda/agenda-armed-contrast.test.ts — real WCAG luminance from the real token values, not hand-copied hex — plus a scan asserting no review.css rule whose selector names a class that appears alongside `chq-btn-primary`/`chq-btn-secondary` in any *.tsx declares `color`. Alternatives foreclosed: bumping specificity on `.chq-btn-primary` (makes every page rule a specificity race) and hard-coding cream in the queue rule (two sources of truth for one face).

## Amendment (wave 41): the OVERALL block prints the plain-average reconciliation line, and the scorecard header carries the queue's own progress

'Averaged by weight · not editable' states the rule but never shows its work, so a reviewer reading 4.5 against scores of 5, 4, 4 has no way to tell a weighted blend from a bug — frame 03--01 answers this with one line between the caption and the value. Ruling: whenever the weighted value is shown (every rating criterion scored), print 'A plain average of <a>, <b>, <c> would be <mean to 2dp>' from the same submitted per-criterion values the weighted blend reads, in criterion order. The weighted math is NOT touched — it is correct and this line exists to defend it. Same stroke: the scorecard header carries the same 'N of N done' progress the queue already computes, read through the SAME progress helper (app/src/pages/review/progress.ts) — one reader, never a second count. aria-pressed on the rating segments stays CLOSED (radio group, aria-checked) and must not be re-filed.

## Amendment (wave 3): scorecard completeness is a LIVE derivation that names its blockers, and a page-level key handler never fires from a form field

The run-4 ABS killer is not a keying drift: Scorecard.tsx latches 'Rate every criterion before submitting.' into `error` state (set at submit, cleared only by the next successful submit), so a reviewer who then fills the gap still reads a refusal that no longer applies — a validation the user cannot clear by fixing the input is a dead screen, and eval agents burned whole turn budgets on it. Second, the page div carries onKeyDown with preventDefault: a digit typed into the comment textarea sets a RATING instead of typing, and Enter submits mid-sentence with the dropdown still empty — which is how the reviewer arrives at the stuck message in the first place. Ruling: ONE predicate, `incompleteCriteria(criteria, scores)`, read by the validator, the notice and the rail markers (isEvaluationComplete becomes its zero-length case, never a second definition); the notice renders only while a submit has been attempted AND blockers remain, and NAMES them; and the keyboard mapper takes the target's kind, returning 'none' for anything originating in input/textarea/select/[contenteditable]. Number-key and Enter shortcuts survive for the page, not for the field.

## Amendment (wave 65): the scorecard is a two-column work surface, and a focus ring is armed by the keyboard, never by page load

DEC-939 clamped the scorecard to `--chq-measure` because the only frames then available were single-column phone captures; the REVIEW PACK's desktop frame 03--01 redraws it as a reading column (~820: eyebrow, title, meta, abstract, form answers) beside a 300px rail carrying the ENTIRE scoring panel — criteria, Overall, comment, recusal, actions. Verified still single-column at app/src/pages/review/Scorecard.tsx:324 (`chq-page chq-review-page chq-measure`). A scorecard is the one screen where you read one thing and act on it repeatedly; splitting reading from acting is what keeps the criteria on screen while the abstract scrolls. Ruling: the scorecard root becomes a two-column grid (reading column + `<aside>` rail) collapsing to one column below ~1200px via a single additive media query; the layout lives in the page's own scorecard.css, not review.css. Second half, closing the USER-FILED P1 verified live at Scorecard.tsx:117-118 + :412 + review.css:549: `focusedId` is pre-armed on load for number-key routing, so the inset ring paints on FIRST PAINT and the top criterion sits in a stray 2px box before any interaction — exactly the "one criterion styled unlike its siblings" defect DEC-939 was written to kill, arriving through the fix rather than the bug. Ruling: keep `focusedId` armed, but gate the `chq-focused` class on a separate ring-armed flag that starts false and is set only by keyboard interaction; the ring rule itself is untouched. Same stroke: the unframed "Tip: number keys 1-9…" hint line is dropped (the REVIEW PACK's supersession note is explicit — no keyboard-tip line, no focus box at rest); the number-key BEHAVIOUR stays.


## Amendment (wave 6): scorecard head and rail anatomy — title alone in the h1, a ref/format/level meta line, a one-line Overall, right-aligned weight, and the queue counter leaves the reading column through a named chrome slot

The wave-72/wave-3 amendments closed the scorecard's load-bearing defects (one scores store, live incomplete derivation, the reading column as the frame's body, the anonymisation notice). ## Amendment (wave 20): a tinted interaction-state band insets its content 16px and keeps the surface's column grid; a row action sizes to its content

The user-filed item in docs/eval-findings.md ("state-band insets") is the ONE mandate entry still open on main: `.chq-review-plan-row.is-active` (app/src/pages/review/review.css:53) paints `background: var(--chq-surface-sunk)` over a row whose padding is `16px 0`, so the tint runs flush to the content on both edges; `.chq-review-reviews-row`/`.chq-review-reviews-detail` (review.css:303-309) set a background with zero padding at all, and `.chq-review-reviews-item-head` free-floats its three cells with `justify-content: space-between`. DEC-939 already owns the interaction-state visual grammar ("focus affordance is an inset ring, never a fill"), so this ruling lands there rather than minting per-surface treatments the user's note explicitly forbids. The rule: a state band is expressed by insetting the tint 16px on the inline axis while the CONTENT stays on the same column positions as its unstated siblings (equal negative margin + padding on a row band; real padding on a colSpan band), and a band's own columns are declared as a grid template, never left to space-between. Second clause, same decision, same file: a row action sizes to its content -- `.chq-review-queue-row-action`'s `min-width: 180px` floor (review.css:1464) invents width the frame does not have; the `max-width: 220px` clamp stays, and any surface that genuinely needs a fixed ~220px outlined action declares it on its own class rather than raising the shared floor.

## Amendment (wave 49): a rating group that claims role=radiogroup owes the radio KEYBOARD contract, not just the roles

Scorecard.tsx:497-522 declares role="radiogroup" with role="radio" buttons and aria-checked, but there is no arrow-key handling and no roving tabindex — every pill is separately tabbable and ArrowRight does nothing. An assistive technology is told this is a radio group and then finds the group's own interaction contract missing; the eval agent that died on CFP-11 reported exactly that ('keyboard (Tab/ArrowRight) can't set them'). Declaring the role is a promise about behaviour. Ruling: roving tabindex (the selected value, or the first when none is selected, is the group's single tab stop) plus Arrow Left/Right/Up/Down moving AND selecting and Home/End jumping to the ends — the standard radio contract. The handler stops propagation so it never double-fires with the page-level number-key scoring (ruling A11, which stays).

## Amendment (wave 3): a button face never appears without the button box — base + face, scan-locked across every SSR view

The wave-39 amendment ruled that a face class owns colour and background. Its dual was never stated: the face classes own ONLY colour/background/border (theme.ts:297-319), while the box — inline-flex, centring, radius, padding, cursor — comes from `.chq-btn` (theme.ts:283-296). Five SSR submits on main name a face with no box: src/routes/account.tsx:126 and src/routes/auth-views.tsx:121, 192, 266, 351, all `className="chq-btn-primary"`. Every one of them is the single primary action on a credential surface. Inside `.chq-auth-card` the omission is masked by a scoped `button[type=submit]` rule; on the bare page nothing masks it, which is how /account/password shipped a 19px primary. This is the same defect kind as the already-fixed contact-drawer Delete ('.chq-btn-tertiary never resets border + missing .chq-btn class'), so it is a class, not an instance. RULING: the button vocabulary is base + face — any element naming `chq-btn-primary`/`chq-btn-secondary`/`chq-btn-tertiary` also names `chq-btn`. Guard: a scan over src/routes/**/*.tsx and src/server/**/*.tsx asserting the two always travel together, so the next hand-written SSR button cannot ship a face with no box.


## Amendment (wave 57) — the scorecard's three tracks sum to the wide measure, and the sum is asserted, not hand-typed

app/src/pages/review/scorecard.css:15-20 lays the scorecard as `minmax(0, 820px) 300px` with `gap: 36px`. 820 + 300 + 36 = 1156. The page root clamps at `--chq-measure-wide` (app/src/styles.css:112 = 1180px). The rule's own comment defends 36 by observing 1156 "fits inside that without a max-width of its own" — fitting inside is not the same as being drawn there, and gate-9 reports the frame's gutter at 60. 820 + 300 + 60 = 1180 EXACTLY. The frame's number is not a ruler reading, it is the arithmetic that makes the two-column work surface fill its declared measure; 36 leaves 24px of unexplained slack and reads as a value fitted to a render, which DEC-989's wave-56 adjudication ("a drawing width is not a measure") does not cover — that ruling protected a landed 820 reading measure from a frame ruler, and here the frame agrees with the token. RULED: gutter 60, and the identity `reading + rail + gap === --chq-measure-wide` becomes an executable assertion that PARSES all four numbers from source, so the three can never drift apart again behind three independently hand-typed literals.


## Amendment (wave 61): every scorecard criterion is its own named group — two criteria that share a label must still be two things to a screen reader

The scorecard's rating rows expose role="radiogroup" with aria-label={criterion.label} and options named bare '1'..'5'. When two criteria carry the same (or near-identical) label, the accessibility tree has nothing left to tell them apart and dedupes them into one set — which is what the a11y audit read as a single 1-5 control governing two rows. An aria-label sourced from user-editable text is not an identity; identity has to come from something the plan editor cannot make collide. Ruling: each criterion renders inside a real fieldset with a visible legend carrying its label, the radiogroup's accessible name is derived from the criterion's own id as well as its label so it is unique by construction, and each role="radio" names both the value and the criterion ('Relevance: 3 of 5') instead of a bare numeral. The same labelled-group treatment covers the dropdown and text criteria. The arrow-key roving-focus handler, the weights and the 820+300+60=1180 grid gutter (DEC-939's wave-57 arithmetic) are untouched.

## Amendment (wave 82): a Choice criterion is a stacked radio row, and an Overall states its denominator

DEC-939 already ruled that a single-select scale is a real `radiogroup` inside a real `<fieldset>/<legend>` with 44px targets (`Scorecard.tsx:558-671`) — and the Choice criterion two branches later (`:673-688`) is a bare `<select>`, the one control on the scorecard that answers to none of that contract. The design ruling gives the reason to stack rather than inline: option labels are words of differing length and a five-across row of them wraps raggedly. Second clause: the Overall caption (`:725-731`) says only "Averaged by weight, not editable" — a number whose denominator is invisible is the thing a reviewer misreads. When the round carries at least one Choice criterion the caption must name what it counted and what it did not ("Weighted mean of the N scored criteria · <Choice label> is recorded, not averaged"). The unweighted-and-excluded arithmetic itself is already correct (`criterionWeightShares` filters to rating) and must not change.


## Amendment (wave 85): a rating criterion at 390 is the frame's full-measure number-chip row — the same vocabulary as desktop, only the geometry differs

Chautauqua Review.dc.html:934-1138 draws each rating criterion's scale as a row of `flex:1; min-height:44px` bordered number chips (`1`..`N`, 16px/600, centred flex, radius 6) rather than the desktop control, and :280-350 draws the same anatomy inside the scored scorecard. This is the exact case DESIGN-RULINGS' density-pairing rule governs — 'colour and weight are identical in both; only geometry differs' — so this is a PAIRED variant of the existing rating control, not a phone-only widget with its own state. It must read the same criterion scale (min/max from the plan's validated scale, per DEC-212's wave-81 amendment) and write through the same handler; a second control with its own value handling is the second-copy-of-a-vocabulary trap. The unscored banner ('One criterion still needs a score' / 'The weighted total cannot be worked out until all three are scored') states the denominator the wave-82 DEC-939 amendment already requires — at 390 it is an ink-outlined card with a 4px left border, not a toast.
