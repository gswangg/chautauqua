# DEC-430: WCAG AA remedies change the pixels, never the instrument's scope: the drag glyph moves to --chq-muted and the public track chip stops using data-supplied colour as a text background

DEC-426's first reading (docs/verification-log/task-w12-c-contrast-pass.md, 35/42) produced exactly two distinct offenders across 7 routes, and each needs a different kind of answer. (1) app/src/pages/forms/forms.css:98-102 draws the drag affordance in var(--chq-border) (#BAB6A6) on paper (#F4F1E8) = 1.80:1 — the hairline/border token exists to draw 1px rules, not glyphs; it moves to var(--chq-muted) (#565A4B, ~7:1). The tempting alternative — teaching scripts/render-sweep-contrast.ts to skip aria-hidden subtrees — is refused: the glyph is visible to sighted low-vision users, and an instrument taught to skip its own findings is precisely the harm DEC-419 forbids. (2) .chq-pub-track-chip (src/routes/public/public.css.ts:116) puts --chq-on-brand text on an ARBITRARY organizer-chosen track colour interpolated inline at src/routes/public/cards.tsx:15; its ratio is therefore a property of DATA (3.00:1 on the seed's amber, 3.10:1 on its green), and no choice of light ink makes it durably AA for colours a producer has not picked yet. The chip therefore stops using the track colour as a text background: ink-on-surface text with a hairline border, and the track colour carried as a bounded swatch fed by a --chq-track-color custom property — contrast becomes a function of tokens alone while SPEC J9's "track colors throughout" is preserved. The colour reaches the style attribute only through the strict DEC-374 hex guard (cards.tsx today interpolates an unguarded string into a style attribute), falling back to the hairline token when it fails.
