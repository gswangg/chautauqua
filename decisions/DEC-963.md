# DEC-963: One SPA calendar-date grammar, and it does not depend on who is reading

app/src/lib/dates.ts:45 already declares the rule in its own header — 'The ONE date grammar in the SPA (DEC-907) -- never toLocaleDateString' — and then two functions in that same module do precisely that. formatDateOnly (:69) returns `new Intl.DateTimeFormat(undefined, {year:'numeric', month:'numeric', day:'numeric'})`, so the SAME timestamp renders '3/2/2026' to an en-US reader and '02/03/2026' to an en-GB one, and formatDateTimeInZone (:163) returns `toLocaleString(undefined, {dateStyle:'medium', timeStyle:'short'})`. Two consequences, both live: the review plan window (PlanList.tsx:35) prints US numeric dates where the design draws '2 Mar – 20 Mar', and the tests that cover these functions (dates.test.ts:87, :184) compute their `expected` value by calling Intl the same way the implementation does — a test that recomputes the implementation cannot fail when the implementation is wrong, which is the enshrined-defect trap the field guide already names. A deterministic sibling, formatDateOnlyLong (:119), has been sitting beside the broken one the whole time. Rule: formatDateOnly renders the house grammar ('2 Mar 2026', UTC-anchored, composed from SHORT_MONTH_NAMES) and its now byte-identical twin is DELETED with its call sites updated — two names for one grammar is one too many; formatDateTimeInZone keeps Intl for the ZONE conversion (it must) but composes the output from formatToParts into the house grammar rather than deferring the format to dateStyle; the two self-recomputing tests assert literal strings; and a scan bans, everywhere under app/src except lib/dates.ts, any toLocale{Date,Time,}String call and any Intl.DateTimeFormat whose options carry dateStyle/timeStyle or (with an implicit locale) day/month/year. A fixed-locale probe and a timeZoneName-only lookup are untouched: the ban is on implicit-locale DATE FORMATTING, not on Intl.
